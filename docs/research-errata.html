ERRATA

Added 12 September 2026

This page corrects four points in the research document. It was added after a review of all project documents against the merged technical reference (SIH26120), which is the source of truth for the model. The pages before this one are unchanged.

1. Viscosity law — Sections 3.4, 3.8, 3.11 and 7.2

Written: ln(μo) = A + B/TK.

Correction: The model uses the ASTM D341 (Walther) form instead: log10(log10(ν + 0.7)) = A − B·log10(T), with T in kelvin, ν in cSt and μ = ν × SG. It is anchored at 11,500 cP at 50 °C (the midpoint of the reported 10,000–13,000 cP), with SG = 0.963, B = 3.5 (assumed) and A = 9.3933. See the technical reference, equation 3. The physical statement T ↑ ⇒ μo ↓ is unchanged.

2. Baseline values — Sections 5.3 and 5.12

Written: the model baseline is 46–48 °C and 17–19° API, from the SIH problem statement.

Correction: The model uses the Oil India field values: native reservoir temperature Ti = 50 °C (the reported bottomhole temperature) and oil SG = 0.963 (from ρo = 963 kg/m³, about 15.4° API). The SIH values are kept for reference, and the difference is cited openly. See the technical reference, section 5, "On the conflicting values".

3. Cause of rod floating — Sections 2.6.4, 2.6.12, 7.6 and 8.3

Written: rod floating is linked to low pump fillage and low reservoir inflow (Section 2.6.4, and Section 2.6.12, causes A to C). Cause C traces high viscosity to floating through lower inflow and pump underfill. Layer 1 is said not to capture rod floating (Sections 7.6 and 8.3).

Correction: Rod floating happens when the rods cannot fall through the oil in the tubing as fast as the polished rod moves down. It occurs when the required fall speed, S·N/30, is greater than the available fall speed, which drops as oil viscosity rises. Its causes are high oil viscosity, high pump speed and a long stroke. Low fillage causes fluid pound, which is a different fault with a different dynamometer-card shape. The two can occur together, but one does not cause the other.

The technical reference (equation 5) computes the floating limit Nmax directly from viscosity, so the physics layer does predict it. Dynamometer-card analysis (Layer 2) is still needed to confirm floating in the field. Section 2.3.14 ("Low fillage should not automatically be labelled rod floating") agrees with this correction.

4. CSS stages — Section 6.10

Written: four explicit stages — injection, soak, production and cooling. Section 3.11 gives the same sequence.

Correction: The model uses three phases: injection, soak and production. Cooling is not a separate stage. It happens during production, and heat lost during soak is included in the thermal efficiency. This agrees with Section 4.6, which says cooling is not necessarily a separate operational step but the natural result of heat input stopping. See the technical reference, equation 7.

Source of truth: merged technical reference (SIH26120), revised 12 September 2026, public/documents/technical-reference.pdf.
